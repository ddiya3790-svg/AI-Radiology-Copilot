BRAIN TUMOR SYMPTOMS

Brain tumor symptoms are not the same for everyone. Symptoms can depend on where a tumor is located, its size, how it affects nearby brain tissue, and how quickly it grows.

Common symptoms that may occur with brain tumors include:

- Headaches, including headaches that are new or becoming worse
- Seizures
- Nausea or vomiting
- Vision problems
- Hearing problems
- Speech or language difficulties
- Problems with balance or walking
- Weakness or changes in movement
- Changes in memory, concentration, mood, personality, or behavior
- Unusual sleepiness or changes in activity level

Important:
These symptoms are not specific to brain tumors. Many other medical conditions can cause headaches, nausea, vision problems, seizures, or changes in concentration.

A person should not conclude that they have a brain tumor simply because they have one or more of these symptoms.

If symptoms are new, persistent, unusual, or getting worse, a healthcare professional should evaluate them.

The location of a tumor can influence which symptoms appear because different parts of the brain control different functions.

The AI system cannot determine whether a person's symptoms are caused by a brain tumor. Symptoms require clinical evaluation by a qualified healthcare professional.
