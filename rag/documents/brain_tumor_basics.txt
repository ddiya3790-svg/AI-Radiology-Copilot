BRAIN TUMOR BASICS
==================

Definition
----------
A brain tumor is a growth of abnormal cells in the tissues of the brain. Brain and central nervous system (CNS) tumors can be benign or malignant.

Primary and Metastatic Brain Tumors
-----------------------------------
A primary brain tumor begins in the brain or another part of the central nervous system. A metastatic brain tumor begins in another part of the body and spreads to the brain.

Brain tumors can differ in their cell type, location, biological behavior, and grade. These differences affect diagnosis, treatment, and prognosis.

Common Brain Tumor Categories
-----------------------------
Examples of primary CNS tumors include:

- Gliomas
- Meningiomas
- Pituitary tumors
- Schwannomas
- Ependymomas
- CNS lymphomas
- Other less common tumor types

Glioma
------
Gliomas are a group of tumors that form in glial cells of the brain and spinal cord. Glial cells support and protect nerve cells.

Gliomas can occur in different parts of the brain or spinal cord. Their biological behavior varies, and some gliomas are low grade while others are high grade.

Meningioma
----------
A meningioma is a primary CNS tumor that forms in the meninges, the layers of tissue covering the brain and spinal cord.

Meningiomas are classified into different grades. Grade 1 meningiomas are generally slower growing, while higher-grade meningiomas can behave more aggressively.

Pituitary Tumor
---------------
A pituitary tumor is a growth of abnormal cells in the pituitary gland. The pituitary gland is a small gland located near the base of the brain and plays an important role in hormone regulation.

Many pituitary tumors are benign. Some pituitary tumors produce excess hormones while others do not.

Symptoms
--------
Symptoms of brain tumors vary depending on factors such as tumor type and location. Possible symptoms can include headaches, seizures, changes in movement or sensation, confusion, and other neurological changes.

Symptoms alone cannot determine the tumor type.

Diagnosis
---------
Diagnosis of a brain tumor may involve medical history, neurological examination, imaging studies such as MRI, and sometimes biopsy or surgical tissue analysis.

MRI can provide detailed images of brain structures, but an MRI image alone does not establish every aspect of a tumor diagnosis.

Important limitation for this AI project
----------------------------------------
The AI Radiology Copilot in this project performs four-class image classification:

- glioma
- meningioma
- notumor
- pituitary

The model prediction is a machine-learning classification result and should not be treated as a confirmed medical diagnosis.

The model does not independently determine:
- tumor grade
- tumor stage
- tumor size
- exact anatomical location
- molecular subtype
- treatment
- prognosis

A qualified healthcare professional and appropriate clinical evaluation are required for diagnosis and medical decision-making.
