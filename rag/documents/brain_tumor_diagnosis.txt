HOW BRAIN TUMORS ARE DIAGNOSED

Diagnosing a brain tumor usually requires more than looking at one MRI image.

A healthcare professional may consider:

- The person's medical history
- Current symptoms
- Neurological examination
- Imaging studies
- MRI scans
- Other tests when appropriate
- Biopsy or surgical tissue examination when needed

MRI is an important imaging method for examining the brain. It can provide detailed images that help doctors identify abnormal areas.

However, an MRI image alone does not necessarily establish the exact type or grade of a tumor.

A biopsy may be used to examine tumor tissue. A pathologist can study the tissue to help determine the tumor type and grade.

The exact diagnostic process depends on the individual situation.

AI IMAGE CLASSIFICATION VS MEDICAL DIAGNOSIS

An AI classification model analyzes image patterns and produces a predicted class.

For example, an AI model may classify an uploaded MRI image as:

- Glioma
- Meningioma
- No Tumor
- Pituitary

This prediction is not equivalent to a medical diagnosis.

The AI model does not replace:

- A radiologist's interpretation
- A neurologist's evaluation
- A neurosurgeon's assessment
- Pathology or biopsy when required
- Other clinical investigations

The AI prediction should therefore be treated as an educational or research result rather than a confirmed diagnosis.
