MRI BASICS
==========

What is MRI?
------------
Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) is a medical imaging technique that uses a strong magnetic field and radio waves to create detailed images of structures inside the body.

MRI is particularly useful for imaging the brain and spinal cord because it provides high-contrast images of soft tissues.

MRI and Brain Imaging
---------------------
Brain MRI can show detailed anatomical structures and can help clinicians evaluate abnormalities such as tumors, bleeding, inflammation, infections, and other neurological conditions.

Different MRI sequences provide different types of information.

Common MRI Sequences
--------------------
T1-weighted MRI:
Provides anatomical detail and commonly shows differences between tissues based on their T1 relaxation properties.

T2-weighted MRI:
Provides strong contrast for tissues containing more water and can make many abnormalities appear relatively bright.

FLAIR:
Fluid-attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR) suppresses the signal from cerebrospinal fluid (CSF). It can make certain abnormalities in the brain more conspicuous.

T1 with contrast:
A contrast-enhanced T1-weighted MRI may be used to evaluate enhancement associated with certain abnormalities. Gadolinium-based contrast agents may be administered when clinically appropriate.

Why MRI is Useful for Brain Tumors
----------------------------------
MRI can provide information about the location and appearance of a brain abnormality and its relationship to surrounding structures.

Radiologists may evaluate features such as:
- lesion location
- signal characteristics
- contrast enhancement
- surrounding edema
- mass effect
- involvement of nearby structures

However, interpretation depends on the complete imaging study, clinical history, and other available information.

MRI Does Not Automatically Establish a Diagnosis
-------------------------------------------------
An abnormal area on MRI does not by itself establish the exact tumor type, grade, or biological behavior.

Definitive diagnosis may require additional clinical evaluation and, in some cases, pathological examination of tissue.

AI and MRI Classification
-------------------------
An AI model can be trained to identify patterns in MRI images associated with predefined classes.

In this project, the classification model predicts one of four classes:

1. Glioma
2. Meningioma
3. No Tumor
4. Pituitary

The model's output represents a learned image-classification prediction.

Important AI limitation
-----------------------
The AI model does not replace radiologist interpretation.

A prediction such as "glioma" means that the image was classified into the glioma class by the trained model. It does not independently confirm that the patient has a glioma.

The model should not be used to determine:
- definitive diagnosis
- tumor grade
- tumor stage
- treatment
- prognosis
- exact tumor measurements
- clinical management decisions

Grad-CAM and MRI
----------------
Grad-CAM (Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping) is an explainability technique that can visualize image regions that contributed to a neural network's prediction.

In this project, Grad-CAM is used to provide a visual explanation of the model's classification.

Grad-CAM should be interpreted as a model-attention explanation, not as a medically validated tumor segmentation or precise tumor boundary.

Educational Use
----------------
This project's MRI analysis system is intended for academic and research purposes.

AI predictions and Grad-CAM visualizations should be reviewed by qualified medical professionals before any clinical interpretation.
